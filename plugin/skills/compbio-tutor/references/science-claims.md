# Science claims

Read this before explaining anything about genetic mapping, genes and disease,
or the mouse insulin secretion study.

## Words to use carefully
- **Associated with / maps to**: a region of the genome whose variation tracks
  with a trait across many animals. This is what QTL mapping finds.
- **Candidate gene**: a gene in that region that might explain the association.
  A hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Validated (in a model)**: an experiment, such as a knockout in mice, changed
  the trait when the gene was changed. It shows the gene *can* affect the trait in
  that model; it does not prove the natural variant in the mapping population
  acts through that gene, and it says nothing direct about people.
- **Causes diabetes**: almost never the right phrase. Type 2 diabetes involves
  many genes and the environment.
- **LOD score**: how much better the data fit with a QTL at that position than
  with none (log10 of the likelihood ratio). Higher means stronger evidence, not
  a bigger effect.

## What the DO islet studies did and did not show
Keller et al. 2018 (Genetics, doi:10.1534/genetics.118.300864) and Keller et al.
2019 (J Clin Invest, doi:10.1172/JCI129143), Attie and Churchill labs.

- About 500 Diversity Outbred mice on a high-fat, high-sucrose diet. Islets were
  taken from each mouse; insulin secretion was measured ex vivo under seven
  conditions (glucose levels, KCl, GLP-1, amino acids, palmitate), and islet gene
  expression was measured by RNA-seq.
- Mapping found many insulin secretion QTL, including hotspots where several
  conditions map together, and condition-specific loci.
- Three candidates were tested in engineered mice:
  - *Hunk* knockout islets secreted more insulin in nearly all conditions, so
    Hunk acts as a negative regulator of secretion in that model. Insulin during
    an oral glucose tolerance test did not differ.
  - *Zfp148* beta-cell knockout islets from diet-fed mice secreted more insulin at
    high glucose (about 3-fold). Total insulin during the glucose tolerance test
    did not differ significantly.
  - *Ptpn18*: mice with an inactive form (knock-in) had better glucose
    tolerance in males on the diet, and their islets secreted less insulin in
    response to amino acids and palmitate.
- The studies used nondiabetic mice and measured islet function. They did not
  show that these genes cause or prevent diabetes in people. Human regions
  matching some loci have been linked to diabetes traits, which makes them worth
  studying.

summary.

## Phrases to avoid
- "the diabetes gene", "the gene for insulin secretion"
- "proves", "causes" (for mapping results)
- "cure", "treatment" (nothing here tests a therapy)
- Claims that the student's own analysis "discovered" a gene: say "your scan
  points to the same region the researchers studied".
